# Hearing Aids: Soundly, AirPods, and How to Not Overpay

*Sister page: [hearing-aids.html](./hearing-aids.html) — same research, linkable tables.*

Researched 2026-09-15 (revised same day after the Sept 9, 2026 Apple event). Two separate decisions, and they are **not** either/or:

1. **Today:** AirPods Pro 3 as an FDA-cleared stop-gap (~$249, returnable).
   Still the current Pro model as of the Sept 9, 2026 event — see the model
   compatibility trap below before you buy anything newer-sounding.
2. **Next few weeks:** a free audiogram via Soundly, which tells you whether #1 is
   actually enough or whether you need prescription aids ($1,998–$4,098/pair).

Do both. #1 does not commit you to anything, and #2 is free.

## The one number that decides everything

Apple's Hearing Aid feature is cleared for **"perceived mild to moderate hearing
loss," adults 18+**. Apple publishes *no dB cutoff* — "perceived" means
self-assessed. The standard clinical categories:

| Degree | dB HL (ASHA/Clark) | AirPods territory? |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | 26–40 | Yes — this is the sweet spot |
| Moderate | 41–55 | Upper edge of cleared range |
| Moderately severe | 56–70 | **No** — prescription aids |
| Severe / profound | 71+ | **No** |

So the whole question is: where do you land? You don't know yet, and neither does
anyone who hasn't tested you. **Nothing below should be read as a guess about your
hearing.** Get the audiogram.

> One audiologist-forum note worth knowing: the Apple test is reported as reliable
> **up to about 55 dB** — which is roughly where "moderate" ends anyway. Past that
> it's both out of clearance range *and* out of measurement range.

## ⚠️ Which AirPods are actually cleared (read before buying)

Only **AirPods Pro 2 and later** — i.e. **Pro 2 and Pro 3**. Apple's own wording:

> "The Hearing Test and Hearing Aid features are supported on **AirPods Pro 2 and
> later** with the latest firmware when paired with a compatible iPhone or iPad
> with iOS 18 or iPadOS 18 and later."
> — [Apple, AirPods Pro Hearing Health](https://www.apple.com/airpods-pro/hearing-health/)

### Not AirPods 5 (announced Sept 9, 2026)
Apple's September 2026 "Surprise and Shine" event shipped **AirPods 5**, not an
AirPods Pro 4. AirPods 5 bring ANC down to the cheap tier ($129, or $149 with
wireless charging + on-stem volume) — but their feature list is Live Listen,
Headphone Accommodations, Headphone Levels, Voice Isolation, Live Translation.
**No Hearing Aid feature, no Hearing Test.** Newer, cheaper, and wrong for this
purpose. "Just buy the latest AirPods" is the trap here.

### Not AirPods 4 with ANC either
Several hearing-aid blogs claim AirPods 4 with ANC are FDA-cleared as OTC hearing
aids. **They are not.** Apple's hearing-health page lists AirPods 4 with ANC only
under *Adaptive Audio* — a different feature entirely. The claim appears to come
from conflating the two. Apple's compatibility footnote is the primary source;
those blogs aren't.

### AirPods Pro 4?
Not announced. As of June 2026 **no FCC filing existed** for one — and Pro 3 was
FCC-approved two months before its launch, so that absence was a real signal. A
September 2026 Pro 4 was an analyst projection (Kuo) that did not materialize at
the event. Rumored whenever it lands: infrared cameras per earbud tied to Visual
Intelligence — a camera/AI upgrade, not a hearing one. **Nothing to wait for.**

## Buying today: AirPods Pro 3

**What to get:** AirPods Pro 3, **$249** — still the current Pro model after the
Sept 2026 event. The Hearing Aid feature costs nothing extra. Pro 2 also works if
you already own a pair; the clearance covers **Pro 2 and Pro 3 only**.

**Setup:** Health app → take the Hearing Test → earbuds auto-tune to your result.

**Return window: 14 days** from Apple (online or retail), free return shipping, no
restocking fee. Original packaging and parts.

> ⚠️ **Timing trap.** The 14-day Apple return window will almost certainly close
> *before* you get a clinic appointment and audiogram. **Buying from Costco (90
> days) solves this** — see "Where to buy" below. So decide on the AirPods on
> their own merits — "do these help me right now, are they worth $249 as earbuds
> plus amplification?" Don't plan to return them based on what the audiologist
> says. At $249 for something you wanted anyway, that's a fine bet — just make it
> knowingly.

### What it genuinely does for you
- FDA-cleared (De Novo, Sept 2024) — first OTC hearing-aid *software* ever cleared.
- **10 hours** in Transparency/hearing-aid mode on Pro 3 (a real jump — 67% more
  than Pro 2).
- Automatic Conversation Boost: amplifies voices, suppresses background.
- **The Hearing Test exports a PDF audiogram** (Health app → Export PDF → Share).
  You can hand that to the clinic. It is *not* a substitute for a real audiogram,
  but it means today's purchase produces actual data instead of just a vibe.
- You can also *import* a professional audiogram later to tune them better.

### Honest limitations (from a hands-on reviewer + audiologist reviews)
- **Amplification only works in Transparency mode.** No ANC + hearing aid at once.
- **10h ≠ all-day.** One reviewer on the 8h figure: *"Compared to the 'all-day'
  battery life of most standard OTC hearing aids, eight hours is a joke."* Real
  aids run ~16h+ or all week on a disposable battery.
- **Own-voice distortion.** Sealed buds make you *"feel somewhat disembodied from
  your own voice."*
- **The core tradeoff is unsolved:** *"nearly impossible to strike a satisfying
  balance between less noise / more amplification."* That reviewer called it a
  deal breaker.
- **Best indoors and quiet.** Poor wind handling outdoors.
- Controls scattered across ~5 different settings screens.
- Visible earbuds in meetings; real aids are near-invisible.
- Verdict from that review: works for **mild** loss, for **part of the day**.

### Ear tips & fit — matters more here than for music

Amplification depends on the **acoustic seal**. A leaky seal bleeds out amplified
sound (especially low frequencies), so a bad fit reads as "the hearing aid feature
doesn't work for me" when the real problem is a tip size. Get this right before
judging the feature.

**Pro 3 ships five sizes: XXS, XS, S, M, L.** XXS is new this generation — Pro 2's
smallest still didn't fit plenty of people.

1. **Run the Acoustic Seal Test first.** New on Pro 3 (replaces the old Ear Tip Fit
   Test): Settings → AirPods → Acoustic Seal Test. Needs iOS 26. Do this **before**
   the Hearing Test — otherwise you calibrate your hearing profile against a bad
   seal and bake the error in.
2. **Expect to size down.** Pro 3 tips are foam-infused silicone and deliberately
   *firmer* than Pro 2's; users consistently report needing a smaller size than
   they used before. Apple says start at medium, then adjust.
3. **Different size per ear is normal.** Ears aren't symmetric. Try it.

⚠️ **Known comfort issue.** Pro 3 has a real complaint trail since launch — sore
ears, irritation, and slipping once sweat or skin oil builds up. The firmer tip
that produces the better seal is the same thing causing the soreness. That's
tolerable for a commute playlist and a much bigger deal for all-day hearing-aid
wear. **Use the 14-day window to test long sessions**, not just an hour at a time.
If no tip size gets you a comfortable all-day seal, that's a genuine point in favor
of custom-molded prescription aids.

### Where to buy — this actually matters

You can buy anywhere; the **return window** is what differs, and it's the whole
ballgame given that a clinic audiogram takes weeks.

| Retailer | Return window | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Costco** | **90 days** | Often bundles **2 years AppleCare+**; seen ~$229. Limit 1/membership. Returnable to any warehouse. |
| Apple (store/online) | 14 days | Free return shipping, no restocking fee. Best for in-person tip fitting + Genius Bar. |
| Best Buy | ~15 days (longer for members) | Price-matches; frequently ~$219–229. |
| Amazon / Walmart | ~30 days | Price-match Costco drops. Buy from the retailer directly, not a marketplace reseller. |

> ✅ **Costco is the strongest option here.** A **90-day** window means you can get
> your Soundly audiogram, test the AirPods against real clinical numbers, and
> *still* return them — which Apple's 14 days does not allow. The bundled
> AppleCare+ covers the case where a tip-fit problem turns into a warranty issue.
> If you have a membership, buy there. This resolves the timing trap above.

If you'd rather have hands-on help sizing ear tips and troubleshooting the Hearing
Test, the Apple Store is worth the shorter window. Avoid third-party marketplace
sellers entirely — counterfeit AirPods are common and won't have the feature at all.

### HSA/FSA — don't count on it
FDA clearance ≠ IRS eligibility. Prescription hearing aids are clearly eligible;
consumer earbuds with amplification modes are generally listed as **not** eligible,
and administrators are waiting on IRS guidance. Ask your plan administrator before
assuming. Prescription aids through Soundly *are* normal eligible medical expenses.

## The Soundly path (free, do it in parallel)

Soundly = transparent fixed pricing on **prescription** aids + a vetted clinic
network. Key perk: **free hearing test and consultation**, no purchase obligation.

- **Provider search:** https://www.soundly.com/provider-search
- **About providers:** https://www.soundly.com/about-our-providers

### Verified current pricing (checked against the live site today)

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Hearing aids, per pair | **$1,998 – $4,098** |
| First 60 days of appointments | **Unlimited, included** |
| Extended care, 1 year | $500 |
| Extended care, 3 years | $1,250 |
| Pay-as-you-go visit | ~$100 |

Soundly claims **25–35% below national average**, and deliberately *unbundles*
care: *"Some programs bundle 2–3 years of care into their pricing. With Soundly,
you only pay for additional care if and when you need it."*

**60-day full-refund return** on the aids themselves — far more forgiving than
Apple's 14 days.

### Process
1. Enter zip at the provider search → browse clinics.
2. Click **"Request an Appointment."** You need the Soundly referral *first* — it's
   what gets you the Soundly price. Don't cold-call the clinic.
3. Referral email with confirmation number goes to you **and** the clinic.
4. You call the clinic to schedule.
5. Test → recommendation → they order for you when ready.
6. Pay through the **Soundly payment portal**, not the clinic.

### Vetting a clinic before you book
- **Audiologist, not just a hearing aid specialist.** Both are licensed; they are
  not the same credential. Soundly's site does *not* surface this distinction —
  you have to check each clinic yourself.
- Confirm they carry the **brand** you're interested in; some clinics are
  effectively single-brand shops.
- Read the clinic's reviews independently.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Today** — Buy AirPods Pro 3 (~$219–249). **Prefer Costco** for the 90-day
   return window + bundled AppleCare+. You want earbuds regardless.
2. **Today** — Run the **Acoustic Seal Test** and settle your tip size *first*,
   then the Health app Hearing Test. Export the PDF audiogram.
3. **Today/tomorrow** — Submit a Soundly appointment request. Free, zero obligation.
4. **Days 1–14** — Actually wear them: meetings, restaurants, outdoors, TV. Note
   where they fail. That's real data for the audiologist.
5. **At the clinic** — Bring the Apple PDF. Get the proper audiogram.
6. **Decide:**
   - Solidly **mild** and AirPods are working → you may be done. Revisit later;
     hearing loss is progressive.
   - **Moderate**, or AirPods fall short in noise/all-day → prescription aids,
     with a 60-day refund window to prove themselves.
   - **Moderately severe or worse** → AirPods were never going to be enough.
     Nothing lost; you got earbuds and a $249 education.

The failure mode to avoid is using a good-enough stop-gap as a reason to skip the
free test for two years. Book the test today too.
