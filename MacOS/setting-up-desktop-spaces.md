Here's how to set up direct keyboard shortcuts to jump to specific Spaces:

- **Step 1**: Create the Spaces first
          Open Mission Control (swipe up with three fingers, or press F3/Ctrl+↑). 
          At the top, click the + button to add as many Spaces as you want. 
          The shortcuts only appear in settings after the Spaces exist.

- **Step 2**: Enable the shortcuts
            Go to `System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Mission Control`. 
            Expand the Mission Control section and you'll see entries like "Switch to Desktop 1," "Switch to Desktop 2," etc., one for each Space you've created.
            By default these are often disabled or unassigned. Check the box next to each one to enable. macOS will assign Ctrl+1, Ctrl+2, Ctrl+3... automatically, or you can click the shortcut field and set your own (e.g., Option+1, Cmd+1 — though Cmd+number conflicts with most apps' tab switching, so Ctrl or Option is safer).
- **Step 3**: Turn off the slow swipe animation (optional but worth it)
            Go to `System Settings → Accessibility → Display` and turn on Reduce motion. This kills the swooshing animation when switching Spaces, making jumps feel instant. Some people love it, some hate losing the animation — try it and see.
- **Step 4**: Pin apps to specific Spaces
            Right-click an app's Dock icon → Options → Assign To → This Desktop. Now that app always opens on that Space. Combined with the shortcuts, "Ctrl+2" effectively becomes "go to my email Space" — which is the naming you were after, just muscle-memory instead of labels.
- **Bonus tip**: Turn off "Automatically rearrange Spaces based on most recent use" in `System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Mission Control`. Otherwise your Spaces shuffle around and Ctrl+3 stops meaning what you expect. Once it's set up, swiping basically becomes obsolete — you just hop directly where you need to go.
